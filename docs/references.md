## ⚓ Case Study 001: Authority Overreach (Knight Capital Group, 2012)

The following public filings, news reports, and government releases document the algorithmic trading incident on August 1, 2012.

### Primary Sources
1.  **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Press Release & Order (2013):**
    *   *Title:* SEC Charges Knight Capital With Violations of Market Access Rule
    *   *URL:* [SEC Press Release 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2013-222)
    *   *Summary:* The SEC's official administrative proceeding detailing how Knight Capital deployed software containing dormant code (Power Peg) that was triggered in production, leading to the execution of 4 million unauthorized stock orders.
2.  **Knight Capital Group, Inc. SEC Filing (Form 10-Q, Q2 2012):**
    *   *Title:* Report for the Quarterly Period Ended June 30, 2012 (Amended / Post-Incident)
    *   *URL:* [Knight Capital Form 10-Q (EDGAR)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1060749/000119312512332176/d391111dex991.htm)
    *   *Summary:* Knight's official financial disclosure filing (Exhibit 99.1) explicitly acknowledging the software deployment malfunction and the resulting pre-tax loss of approximately $440 million.

### Secondary Sources
3.  **Reuters Financial Market Coverage:**
    *   *Title:* Knight trading loss shows cracks in equity markets
    *   *URL:* [Reuters Market Report (August 2012)](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/knight-trading-loss-shows-cracks-in-equity-markets-idUSBRE87203X/)
    *   *Summary:* Detailed contemporary reporting on the 45-minute execution window, trader reactions, and the systemic market impacts of the massive position accumulation.
4.  **Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago (Chicago Fed):**
    *   *Title:* The Knight Capital Group Incident: Systemic Risks in Algorithmic Trading
    *   *URL:* [Chicago Fed Policy Discussion Archive](https://www.chicagofed.org/) *(Search: Knight Capital Group)*
    *   *Summary:* Academic and policy analysis of automated trading limits and the failure of post-execution mitigation boundaries.
